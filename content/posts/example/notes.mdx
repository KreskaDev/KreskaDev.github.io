# Notes — extended reference

This page is a **subpage** of the [Example Post playbook](/posts/example). It demonstrates two things at once:

1. The supporting-page route `/posts/<slug>/<filename>/` (here: `/posts/example/notes`)
2. The `PostBreadcrumb` component, automatically rendered at the top of every subpage to link back to the parent post

The ContentNavigator works on subpages too — scroll down and the TOC on the right tracks your position, just like on the main post.

## When to use a subpage

Subpages are for **deep references** that would clutter the main post if inlined: full mathematical derivations, glossaries, FAQ-style addenda, or extended bibliographies. The math derivation under [`/posts/pozytywny-wynik/math`](/posts/pozytywny-wynik/math) is the canonical example in this blog.

Anchor links from the main post into a subpage work the usual way — `[derivation](/posts/example/notes#derivations)`. The browser handles the cross-page jump natively; `scroll-padding-top` ensures the target heading lands below the TopNav.

## Frontmatter schema

Subpages use a smaller schema than the main post — three fields, none of them surfacing in blog lists:

```yaml
---
title: Notes — extended reference
parent: example
language: en
---
```

The `parent` field is required and must match a post slug. It feeds the breadcrumb label at the top of this page and the `<link rel="canonical">` in `<head>`.

Subpages are **not** indexed in the blog list (`/blog`) nor in the search index — they are discoverable only via inline links from their parent post.

## Derivations

This section exists to give the in-page TOC a few headings to track. Scroll up and down and watch the right-hand nav follow your position.

### Step one

Start with the joint probability:

$$
P(H, E) = P(E \mid H) \cdot P(H)
$$

### Step two

Apply the definition of conditional probability:

$$
P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(H, E)}{P(E)}
$$

### Step three

Substitute and rearrange:

$$
P(H \mid E) = \frac{P(E \mid H) \cdot P(H)}{P(E)}
$$

This is Bayes' theorem in its compact form. The denominator expands via the law of total probability:

$$
P(E) = P(E \mid H) \cdot P(H) + P(E \mid \neg H) \cdot P(\neg H)
$$

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Prior     | $P(H)$ — belief about $H$ before observing $E$ |
| Likelihood | $P(E \mid H)$ — probability of observing $E$ given $H$ |
| Posterior | $P(H \mid E)$ — belief about $H$ after observing $E$ |
| Evidence  | $P(E)$ — marginal probability of $E$ across all hypotheses |
